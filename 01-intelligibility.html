Intelligibility: what becomes enough?

Home · Next

An intelligibility is a relational organization sufficiently determinate to be taken up as a whole for a relevant demand. Truth, grammatical completeness, and intelligibility are different: a false statement may be perfectly intelligible; a true statement may not yet be understood.

Principle of sufficient constraint (proposed): an unfolding event becomes complete when its governing unresolvedness is constrained enough for determinate uptake, without requiring all future continuation to cease.

The crucial word is enough. Moving boxes can improve a passage without making it passable. The last obstruction's removal may be the decisive threshold. The governing demand determines what counts as completion.

Three questions follow: What insufficiency is overcome? What makes that overcoming sufficient? What remains achieved? These motivate the three axes, but do not prove that exactly 54 forms exist.
