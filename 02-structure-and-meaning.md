# Structure, meaning, and achieved intelligibility
[Home](index.html) · [Previous](01-intelligibility.md) · [Next](03-the-sentence.md)

**Structure** is the network of grammatical, sequential, referential and pragmatic constraints by which parts bear on one another. **Meaning** is what those parts, so organized, can signify, assert, question or make available. **Intelligibility** is the achieved possibility of grasping that meaningful organization as one sufficient act.

“Between structure and meaning” names an explanatory relation, not an extra substance or an interval in which meaning is manufactured.

“She put it there” is grammatically complete but may lack recoverable referents. “Tomorrow” can be a complete reply to “When is the hearing?” The garden-path sentence “The horse raced past the barn fell” makes earlier material acquire new grammatical roles at the last verb.

The Operative Centre identifies what governs this achieved coherence; it is not synonymous with the final word or the grammatical head.
