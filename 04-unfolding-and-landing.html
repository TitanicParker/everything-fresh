Sentence unfolding, governance, and landing

Home · Previous · Next

Each new phrase changes the field of coherent interpretations. A noun phrase can close locally before the sentence's governing act completes. A later negation, condition or explanatory clause can reorganize earlier material.

Landing names the point or span at which the governing organization becomes sufficiently determinate. It need not be punctuation or the last word.

Four tests help locate governance:

  1. Scope: Which relation governs the whole, rather than a subordinate operation?
  2. Retrospective control: Which material fixes the interpretation of earlier words?
  3. Uptake: What must be recovered for the sentence to count as this act?
  4. Counterfactual: Would removing the alleged centre leave the same completion intact?

A centre can be distributed. Local closure and governing completion must not be confused.
