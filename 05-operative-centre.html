The Operative Centre

Home · Previous · Next

Foundational proposal: “The minimal governing determination through which an unfolding intelligibility becomes sufficient and holds as achieved.”

The Centre is not a fourth axis, a metaphysical object inside a sentence, a grammatical head, or necessarily one word. It can be a dependency or distributed organization.

Constructive refinement: identify (1) the governing demand, (2) the indispensable organization, (3) the sufficiency condition, and (4) the achieved result.

unresolved demand —[governing organization meets sufficiency]→ achieved intelligibility

For “The shelf holds weight because its parts brace one another,” the operative determination is the mutually supporting role-organization that explains joint capacity, not the noun shelf.

Counterfactual test: if a proposed centre can be removed without changing the governing achievement, it is not indispensable. If two competing centres remain equally viable, record ambiguity.

The three axes are simultaneous constraints on this single achievement, not steps of its temporal construction.
