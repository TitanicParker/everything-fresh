Gate One — source analysis

Every coastline is a temporary agreement between restless water and resistant land.

Unfolding and candidates

“Every coastline” identifies the scope. “Temporary agreement” characterises each boundary as provisional, rather than fixed independently. “Between restless water and resistant land” specifies its counterpart conditions. “Agreement” is figurative; no consciousness, contract or consent is attributed to water and land.

  1. Coastlines exist where land meets water. Loses provisionality and the contrasting contributions.
  2. Water defeats land and moves the coastline inland. Adds a direction and an outcome.
  3. Every coastline is a provisional boundary constituted through restless water meeting resistant land. Preserves the temporary arrangement and both counterparts.
  4. Water and land form a self-maintaining whole. Adds systemic stability and self-maintenance.

Candidate 3 survives. “Temporary” does not supply a duration or a prediction of a particular future movement.

Four holding tests

  1. Identity: a provisional water–land boundary, not a permanent coordinate or a literal contract.
  2. Constitutive dependencies: water and land are constituents of the boundary's identity, not merely causes or evidence encountered earlier.
  3. Operative continuation: the result is characterised as temporary, but no specific changed course, movement or accomplished control is established.
  4. Minimal invalidation: leave a mapped line while removing the opposed meeting; the geographical boundary described is no longer constituted there.

Achieved-identity discrimination

What has been established is: every coastline is a provisional arrangement between water and land.

What obtains through what is: the coastline's provisional boundary obtains through restless water meeting resistant land.

The second explains the identity of the boundary, rather than merely supplying a reason to believe a line exists. A detached line can remain drawn after its boundary relation changes; it would no longer be the same constituted coastline. The achievement does not require literal equality of forces or stable equilibrium.
