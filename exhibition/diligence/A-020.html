Gate One — source analysis

The world's languages divide experience differently while helping people recognize familiar situations.

Unfolding and candidates

“The world's languages” supplies a plurality. “Divide experience differently” makes their articulations divergent. “While helping people recognize familiar situations” places recognitive usefulness alongside that divergence.

The completed point holds variation in division together with recognition of the familiar. It neither makes language an impermeable prison nor asserts that all speakers divide experience alike. “Familiar” does not establish identical interpretations, identical referents for every speaker, or universal mutual translation.

  1. Languages classify experience differently. Omits recognition.
  2. All languages express exactly the same meanings. Contradicts or flattens the stated differences.
  3. Languages' differing divisions of experience coexist with their helping people recognise familiar situations. Preserves both sides without erasing either.
  4. Different categories cause people to recognise identical situations in identical ways. Adds causation and universal identity.
  5. Language has newly given people a capacity they previously lacked. Adds a before/after acquisition.

Candidate 3 is strongest. The helping function is retained within the contrast; it is not denied because the whole concerns their coexistence.

Four holding tests

  1. Identity: linguistic difference understood alongside recognition of the familiar; neither bare diversity nor bare usefulness.
  2. Constitutive dependencies: both sides give the contrast its identity. Without the recognitive side, difference loses this specific counterpoint; without difference, recognition loses this particular setting.
  3. Operative continuation: a helping function is asserted and preserved, but the governing completion is not acquisition of a new ability or an ensuing sequence of recognition. No universal recognition or translation success is added.
  4. Minimal invalidation: replace helping with preventing recognition; both language and diversity can remain while this contrasted coexistence fails.

Achieved-identity discrimination

The whole is not merely two independent facts: their coexistence distinguishes variation in articulation from loss of recognisability. Their contextual contrast constitutes this particular significance. No physical sustaining dependence or causal inference is required.
