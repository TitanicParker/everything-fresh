Gate One — source analysis

A lighthouse matters most when darkness makes the coastline difficult to distinguish.

Unfolding and candidate Centres

A lighthouse is said to matter “most,” leaving the condition of greatest significance to be specified. “When darkness” introduces that condition, and the difficulty of distinguishing the coastline gives its particular content.

  1. A lighthouse exists in darkness. Loses comparative importance and the stated difficulty.
  2. A lighthouse always makes the coastline completely visible. Adds successful illumination and universality.
  3. A lighthouse has greatest significance under darkness-induced difficulty in distinguishing the coastline. Preserves importance, its maximum and the specifying condition.
  4. A ship avoids collision because of the lighthouse. Adds ship, action and successful outcome.

Candidate 3 survives. “Most” is not “only”; importance under other conditions is not denied. “Difficult” is not “impossible.”

Four holding tests

  1. Identity: condition-relative greatest significance, not bare existence or performed guidance.
  2. Constitutive dependencies: the difficulty specifies what this heightened importance consists in. Remove that condition and this particular importance attribution loses its identity.
  3. Operative continuation: practical significance is asserted, but no completed navigation, visual success or newly achieved capacity is identified. Plausible usefulness is not substituted for such an achievement.
  4. Minimal invalidation: retain difficulty while remove the lighthouse's heightened importance under it.

Achieved-identity discrimination
