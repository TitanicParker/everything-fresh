Gate One — source analysis

A new road shortens journeys while changing which towns people pass through.

Unfolding and candidate Centres

“A new road” identifies a newly introduced source of change. “Shortens journeys” establishes one consequence. “While changing which towns people pass through” retains that consequence and specifies another aspect of the travel changed by the same road. The completed point is not travel improvement alone: shortening comes together with altered passage through places.

  1. The road shortens journeys. Omits the changed towns.
  2. The road destroys the towns it bypasses. Adds bypass, destruction and a social outcome.
  3. The new road changes travel jointly through shorter journeys and different towns passed through. Preserves both stated consequences.
  4. The road is built in order to redirect economic activity. Invents purpose and an economic mechanism.

Candidate 3 survives. “Shortens” is not silently converted into a measured distance or travel-time claim. No universal benefit, particular town or actual traffic volume is asserted.

Four holding tests

  1. Identity: one source's joint travel consequences, not a generic advantage or two unrelated facts.
  2. Constitutive dependencies: the road and both consequences identify the particular influence. No exclusive cause, feedback or consequence-to-consequence dependency is asserted.
  3. Operative continuation: yes; changed journeys and passage are expressly the differences established, rather than optional applications of an otherwise complete description.
  4. Minimal invalidation: shorter journeys occur but the towns passed through do not change; the full influence attributed is not established.

Achieved-identity discrimination

What has been established is: the new road shortens journeys and changes the towns passed through.

What obtains through what is: those joint changes in travel occur through the new road's influence.

The achieved identity is consequential change, not merely an established association or a static description held through another term. Both consequences must remain, but the record does not add coupling between them or a broader circulation mechanism.
