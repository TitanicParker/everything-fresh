Gate One — source analysis

The earth beneath a playground contains histories no child needs to know.

Unfolding and candidate Centres

The earth is located beneath a playground. It contains histories, giving the present site a past depth. The relative clause specifies those histories as ones no child needs to know. Existence of this past is therefore not equated with a requirement that children know it.

  1. The ground contains traces of history. Omits the children's lack of need to know.
  2. Children know nothing about the past. Changes unnecessary knowledge into actual ignorance.
  3. The ground beneath a playground contains histories without any child's needing to know those histories. Preserves presence, setting and non-necessity.
  4. Children must be protected from disturbing archaeological facts. Adds a protective purpose, disturbing content and an injunction.

Candidate 3 survives. “No child needs to know” neither says no child does know nor prohibits learning. The sentence does not give a particular past event, claim a physical artefact, or explicitly report children playing.

Four holding tests

  1. Identity: historical presence distinguished from epistemic necessity, not an account of actual ignorance.
  2. Constitutive dependencies: both terms identify this distinction, but the histories are not constituted through children's ignorance or a continuing contrast. The result is the determination that knowing is unnecessary.
  3. Operative continuation: no accomplished ability to play without knowledge or new permission to ignore the past is established. The lack of need is not a reported liberation from a prior rule.
  4. Minimal invalidation: retain the histories but require their knowledge for a child; the universal lack of need is defeated.

Achieved-identity discrimination

What has been established is: these histories exist without any child's needing to know them.

What obtains through what is: the distinction is specified by considering their presence alongside children's need to know.

The second specifies the result rather than supplying an ongoing support for it. A statement of contextual poignancy might be available, but the necessary achieved determination is non-necessity of knowing, not histories made meaningful only through ignorance. No previous knowing-demand is stated as having governed and then been removed.
