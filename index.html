The Operative Centre of Intelligibility

When do words become enough?

A sentence unfolds word by word. Its relations gather force. Then something becomes sufficiently determinate to be understood as one whole. What governs that achievement?

The central idea

The sentence as unitus

A completed sentence is a unit of intelligibility. Its Operative Centre is the minimal governing determination through which its unfolding organization becomes sufficient and holds as achieved. The centre may be a word, a dependency, a relation or a distributed configuration—not necessarily the final word.

One governing achievement

Not three labels pasted onto a sentence.

The three dimensions coincide in the same completion-event: what unresolvedness changes, what makes the change enough, and how the result remains available.

Words unfold→Centre becomes recognizable→Whole is sufficient→Three dimensions coincide

This is a route of recognition, not a temporal assembly of the three dimensions.
