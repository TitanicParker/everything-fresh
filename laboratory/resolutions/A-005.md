## Gate One — source analysis and freeze

### Exact source and unfolding

> Cities grow by making strangers useful to one another without introductions.

**Provenance:** A-005, frozen Corpus A §15, Edition 1.1 workbook, original item 5; source file `One_Hundred_Statements_About_the_World(4).md`; workbook blob `1b511a439a5007a3c4a61c36ff5417f415005e2c`. Source matched verbatim. The generic plural is preserved without adding a claim that all urban growth has this single cause.

“Cities grow” supplies the result, leaving its asserted means unresolved. “By making strangers useful to one another” makes the growth intelligible through the establishment of practical usefulness among people who need not know each other. “Without introductions” specifies that this usefulness does not require an introductory encounter. The result is a way urban growth proceeds through effective connections between strangers, not just the observation that cities contain unfamiliar people.

No particular service, market, institution, division of labour, coordinated plan or measure of growth is specified. “Useful to one another” is retained at the level of the social field; it does not imply that every pair exchanges equal benefits, or that every stranger benefits every other.

### Verification tests

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| Deletion | Delete the means: an unexplained growth report survives, but not this achievement. Delete growth: useful connections remain, but their role in urban growth disappears. Delete the introduction qualification: the means remains but its specific independence from introductions is lost. |
| Alteration | Replace practical usefulness with mere co-presence: growth is no longer explained by what strangers can do for one another. Replace “by” with mere accompaniment: the asserted contribution to growth weakens. |
| Narrowing | Require people already to know each other: the stranger-based achievement changes. Replace general urban growth with one named service: the source's scope narrows. |
| Reversal | Make usefulness require introductions: the specific independence is defeated. Make strangers useless to one another while preserving city growth: the source's stated way growth proceeds is lost. |

| Sufficiency-location | The completed means lies across the people becoming useful to one another and its contribution to the city's growth. An isolated stranger or city name does not suffice; no privileged coordinating institution is specified. |

| Operative test | Retain unfamiliarity and connections but remove usefulness: the practical contribution to growth fails. A bare relational configuration is insufficient. |

### Four sharpened holding tests

1. **Identity:** Urban growth proceeding through practical usefulness established among strangers, independently of introductions. The achievement is a consequential way the urban field operates, not simply a collection of social positions.
2. **Constitutive dependencies:** The growth-means connection, usefulness among strangers and absence of an introduction prerequisite identify this particular achievement. Removing any changes it. No equal mutual benefit or universal pairwise exchange is added.
3. **Operative continuation:** Yes. The asserted result specifies how a city grows: connections make strangers practically consequential for one another. This consequence is part of what the sentence states, not an inference from general usefulness of a proposition.
4. **Minimal invalidation:** Keep urban growth and social contact but remove strangers' usefulness as a contributing means. Growth might remain, but this achieved way of proceeding fails. Requiring introductions separately defeats its independence qualification.

### Achieved-identity discrimination

**Result formulation:** What has been established is that cities grow through making strangers useful to one another without requiring introductions.

**Constitution formulation:** What obtains through what is cities' growth through the practical usefulness they establish among people who do not need introductory encounters.

Both formulations preserve the full statement, but a distinction remains between the retained dependence and the consequential work it does. The source establishes not merely that the people have related standings: their usefulness is a means through which growth proceeds.

Further pre-freeze tests:

- Keep people linked but make those links practically useless: an arrangement remains, but the governing contribution fails.
- Keep usefulness dependent on introductions: a different way of operating remains, but the particular independence is lost.
- Keep introduction-independence but remove any prior rule requiring introductions: nothing is lost; a former governor's removal is not needed.
- Keep practical usefulness and growth while omit a detailed allocation of roles: the achievement survives; a planned organisation or complementary task structure is unnecessary.
- Retain the complete growth-means dependency as a proposition but deny any practical usefulness contributing to growth: the content itself is contradicted, not merely deprived of an application.

**Discrimination outcome:** The identity retained is an operative contribution to growth. Its dependencies are indispensable, but the contribution cannot be exhausted by the fact that they constitute a relation. It is also not an assertion that the utterance itself causes urban growth.
