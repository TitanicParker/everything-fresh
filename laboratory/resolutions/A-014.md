## Gate One — source analysis

> Even broken pottery tells archaeologists how ordinary hands once held their world.

### Unfolding

“Even broken pottery” makes damage an insufficient reason to dismiss the material. “Tells archaeologists” establishes its informative role. The final contribution specifies the content: how ordinary people once handled or practically inhabited their world. “Hands” carries the human activity figuratively; “held their world” is not restricted to literal gripping.

The source establishes surviving pottery as an informative trace of past ordinary life. It does not establish that a particular discovery has already occurred, that every shard is informative, or that the full past can be reconstructed.

### Competing Centres

1. Broken pottery exists. Omits the informative achievement.
2. Archaeologists can reconstruct all of ancient society from a shard. Adds completeness, scale and a particular object.
3. Broken pottery remains informative to archaeologists about ordinary people's past practical engagement with their world. Preserves damage, informative source, recipient and content.
4. Pottery is repaired so that its historical meaning returns. Invents repair; brokenness is retained.

Candidate 3 survives. The source supplies an evidential bearing, rather than merely an unrestricted possibility of research.
