## Gate One — source analysis

> Every map leaves something out so that something else becomes easier to find.

### Unfolding and candidate Centres

“Every map” sets the scope. “Leaves something out” establishes selectivity rather than exhaustive representation. “So that” gives that omission an enabling purpose or function. “Something else becomes easier to find” specifies improved findability, not simply absence.

1. Maps are incomplete. Incomplete: loses the omission's enabling function.
2. Everything omitted is worthless. Invents a value judgment.
3. Every map's selective omission serves to make other content easier to find. Preserves scope, means and improvement.
4. A map guarantees every search succeeds. Invents universal success and enlarges “something else” to everything.

Candidate 3 survives. The generic functional formulation preserves “so that” without requiring a named user's actual successful search or an explicit cartographer's mental intention.

### Verification tests

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|

| Alteration | “Harder to find” reverses the effect. “Everything else” overstates the beneficiary range. |
| Narrowing | One particular map would lose the universal scope. One named omitted item would add a specificity not supplied. |

### Four holding tests

1. **Identity:** an enabling function of selective presentation, not mere incompleteness or a completed search.
2. **Constitutive dependencies:** omission is the presented means. A detached omission or an independently easier search would omit this specified function.
3. **Operative continuation:** yes; easier finding is expressly the function made available. Actual successful exercise is unnecessary, and no particular searcher is asserted.
4. **Minimal invalidation:** preserve selective absence while remove its facilitation of other finding.

### Achieved-identity discrimination

**What has been established is:** every map's omission serves improved findability of other content.

**What obtains through what is:** easier finding is enabled through selective presentation.

The relation is necessary, but the achieved identity is its practical facilitation of finding. Neither a merely established absence nor a conditional description without that facilitation captures the completion. This function is explicit rather than inferred from general usefulness of maps.
