## Gate One — source analysis

> When a language disappears, particular ways of noticing may become harder to inherit.

### Unfolding and candidate Centres

“When a language disappears” specifies a condition. “Particular ways of noticing” identifies what may be affected, without extending to every way of thinking. “May become harder to inherit” establishes a possible worsening of transmission, not inevitable impossibility or a reported actual loss in one case.

1. A language disappears. Omits what this can mean for transmission.
2. All thought associated with a lost language vanishes irretrievably. Adds universal scope, certainty and impossibility.
3. A language's disappearance may make the inheritance of particular ways of noticing more difficult. Preserves condition, selected content, possibility and increased difficulty.
4. Losing a language creates new abilities to notice. Reverses the direction of the consequence.

Candidate 3 survives. “Inherit” concerns cultural transmission here, not a biological genetic mechanism. No particular language, community or generation is named.

### Verification tests

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|

| Alteration | “Impossible” overstates “harder”; “will” overstates “may”; “all ways” overstates “particular.” |
| Narrowing | A named linguistic community would add specificity, while one specified technique of noticing would add content absent from the source. |
| Reversal | Easier inheritance reverses the stated tendency. Difficult inheritance causing language disappearance changes the direction of the conditional account. |

| Minimal invalidation | Under disappearance, the stated increase in inheritance difficulty is not even possible: the attributed potential consequence fails. |

### Four holding tests

1. **Identity:** a scoped possible impairment of transmission, not complete loss of thought or inevitable failure.
2. **Constitutive dependencies:** the consequence is specified through disappearance; alternatives for transmission are not ruled out. Both source condition and selected inheritance identify the potential influence.
3. **Operative continuation:** yes; increased difficulty in what can be passed on is explicitly the possible consequence. No actual failed inheritance is required to establish the scoped potential.
4. **Minimal invalidation:** deny the possibility of the particular increased difficulty under disappearance while retaining the condition and selected content.

### Achieved-identity discrimination

**What has been established is:** disappearance may make selected forms of noticing harder to inherit.

**What obtains through what is:** possible impairment of transmission follows through the loss of the language.

The result's identity lies in what may change for transmission, rather than a bare established condition or a static description of one term through another. The consequence can be adverse and remain genuinely consequential. No beneficial opening, new permission or performed failure is added.
