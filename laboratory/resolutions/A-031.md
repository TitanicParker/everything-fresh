### Unfolding and candidate Centres

“A judge's decision” identifies a determination. “Can outlast” attributes possible persistence beyond another thing. “The room where it was first spoken” supplies the physical setting of its initial utterance as the comparative term.

1. A judge makes a decision. Omits possible persistence beyond the room.
2. Every judicial ruling remains legally binding forever. Adds universality, legal force and unending duration.
3. A judge's decision can persist beyond the room of its first utterance. Preserves possibility, comparative duration and initial setting.
4. Destroying a room makes a decision valid. Invents destruction as a cause and legal validity as an outcome.

Candidate 3 survives. The sentence compares possible endurance; it does not report an actual demolition, recitation, enforcement or transmission mechanism.
