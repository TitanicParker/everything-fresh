## Gate One — source analysis

> The mountain does not move, but every climber discovers a different approach.

### Unfolding and candidate Centres

The mountain does not move. “But” asks that this constancy be understood alongside a contrasting contribution. Each climber discovers a different approach. The same object can remain fixed while ways of approaching it differ in the climbers' discoveries.

1. The mountain stays still. Omits the discovery of differing approaches.
2. Climbers physically change the mountain. Contradicts the supplied constancy.
3. The mountain remains fixed while each climber discovers a different way of approaching it, distinguishing object constancy from variation in approach. Preserves the whole contrast.
4. Every climber creates a physically unique route that no one else could use. Adds creation, exclusive ownership and a uniqueness criterion.

Candidate 3 survives. “Approach” can concern a way toward or of engaging the mountain. No narrow physical route geometry, purely psychological theory or mathematical proof that all routes are pairwise unique is required to preserve the governing contrast. “Discovers” is not silently replaced with “creates.”

### Verification tests

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| Deletion | Remove the mountain's stillness: varied discoveries remain without the contrast of a constant object. Remove the climbers: physical constancy remains without varied approach. |
| Alteration | Make all discoveries the same approach: variation is lost. Make the mountain move to offer each way: variation no longer coexists with the supplied fixed object. |
| Narrowing | One climber alone does not preserve the statement's across-climber variation. A specific route or psychological interpretation would add an unnecessary restriction. |
| Reversal | A changing mountain with an unchanging approach exchanges which aspect varies and establishes a different point. |
| Dependency | Approach variation is distinguished from object movement. The mountain is not constituted as unmoving through comparison, and an approach need not be brought into existence by its discovery. |
| Sufficiency-location | The distinction requires the same mountain and differing climber approaches across the cases. Neither an isolated still mountain nor one isolated discovery establishes it. |
| Availability test | No prior impasse, inaccessible mountain or newly enlarged stock of routes is asserted. Discovery of difference is not itself a claim that the mountain has acquired more routes. |
| Minimal invalidation | Retain the fixed mountain but remove variation among the approaches discovered; the complete contrast fails. |

### Six confirmation questions

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What was unresolved? | Whether constancy of the mountain coincides with constancy of ways of approaching it. |
| What changed? | The mountain's fixity and varied approaches are distinguished while kept in one account. |
| What made it sufficient? | The unchanged object is retained across the climbers' differing discoveries. |
| Where is sufficiency located? | Across the constant object and variation among the approaches. |
| What remains achieved? | The distinction between fixity of the mountain and variability of approaching it. |
| What would invalidate it? | The approaches cease to differ while the mountain remains fixed. |

### Four holding tests

1. **Identity:** an established contrast of object constancy with approach variation, including discoveries by the climbers.
2. **Constitutive dependencies:** both aspects identify this contrast, but neither constancy nor the existence of approaches is constituted through a sustaining counterpart. Discovery does not entail creation.
3. **Operative continuation:** discovery is included, but no newly enabled route, improved climbing capacity, successful ascent or changed subsequent conduct is established.
4. **Minimal invalidation:** retain the unmoving object while the approaches discovered no longer differ; one side remains without the contrasted result.

### Achieved-identity discrimination

**What has been established is:** a fixed mountain coexists with differing approaches discovered by climbers.

**What obtains through what is:** the distinction is specified through comparing constant object with variable approach.

The comparison establishes the distinction rather than sustaining either term's identity through the other. An alternative claim that each approach exists only through a climber's perspective would add constitution beyond discovery. The achieved result is the distinction left determined, not a theory of observer-created reality.
