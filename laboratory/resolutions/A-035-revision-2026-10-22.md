# A-035 — dialogue revision, 22 October 2026

**Sentence:** “The same rain nourishes one field and washes another field away.”

**Status:** Dialogue-selected resolution; supersedes the first-pass *candidate* status for current working use, while preserving the original [first-pass investigation](A-035.md) and its unresolved audit questions.

**Selected cell:** **06 — Field separation — “These among these.”** (Differentiation × Distributed × Efficacy).

**Traffic cop / governing construction:** “the same … one … another,” with distinct predicates of effect. No single verb alone supplies the classification.

**Operative Centre:** Under a common influence, different recipients undergo distinct effects.

**The structural achievement:** The same rain supplies a common condition; the two fields are the distinct recipients, and different effects are asserted of them. Differentiation is operative across the recipients. Distributed is not justified by simply counting two fields: the differentiated pattern requires comparison across them. Efficacy concerns the different effects attributed to the recipients, not merely the existence of multiple nouns.

**Correction from user-led counterfactual testing:** The initial discussion overread the *positive/negative polarity* of “nourishes” versus “washes away” as structural evidence. That polarity is not necessary. “The same rain nourishes one field and turns another field yellow” retains the operative differentiation without opposed evaluations. “The same rain nourishes one field and nourishes another” retains two recipients but no stated differentiation in their effects. Do not infer unstated causes such as soil, slope, drainage or topography. The canonical name “Field separation” coinciding with literal fields is memorable but is **not** classification evidence.

**Rivals:** 22 — Sequential link — “Then.” (Relating × Distributed × Standing) fails because no ordering or sequence is asserted. 03 — Boundary act — “This far.” (Differentiation × Focal × Efficacy) lacks a focal threshold. 09 — Exclusionary override — “Not by that rule.” (Differentiation × Release × Efficacy) lacks displacement of an inherited rule. 05 — Context-bound mark — “This here.” (Differentiation × Distributed × Bearing) remains a serious neighbouring test: does the sentence establish different operative effects or only context-relative standing? Here the explicit effect predicates favour 06, but a unique-cell proof is not claimed.

**Methodological rule:** Never promote semantic polarity, evaluative connotations, coincidental cell names, or a mere count of recipients into the governing structure. Use controlled substitutions to identify what the construction must preserve.
