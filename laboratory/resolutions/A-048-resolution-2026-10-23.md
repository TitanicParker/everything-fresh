# A-048 — owner-selected Cell 04 (23 October 2026)

**Corpus sentence (unchanged):** A song crossing generations may retain its shape while gathering new associations.

**Gate One — Operative Centre:** A song's form may remain invariant across generations even as associations gathered around it change. The operative achievement is the possible standing of identity through temporal distribution, not simply the existence of two parallel predicates.

**Gate Two — Cell 04, Scope standing:** **Differentiation × Distributed × Standing**.

- **Differentiation:** “A song crossing generations” restricts the claim to this kind of song rather than all songs.
- **Distributed:** “Crossing generations” distributes the invariant across distinct times/generations. Distribution does not require multiple grammatical subjects: the same song spans temporal positions.
- **Standing:** “May retain its shape” asserts the possibility of the song's form standing through that span. The modal “may” is preserved; the sentence does not promise that every such song does so.
- **“While gathering new associations”:** supplies the changing interpretive surroundings alongside which the invariant can stand. Coexistence, not necessarily opposition.

**Rivals considered:** Settlement × Focal × Efficacy was initially proposed, by analogy with A-047. It captures the single subject and an apparent capacity, but does less justice to the temporal distribution of an invariant and the modal standing of retained form. A Focal reading remains structurally imaginable; owner and assistant settled on the stronger temporal-distribution account in dialogue. This is an **owner-selected resolution**, not an independent proof of uniqueness.

**Owner's decisive formulation:** “crossing generations is evidently distributive of the invariant, it spans time”.

**Status:** accepted in dialogue and formally recorded. **Next:** A-049.
