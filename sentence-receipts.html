The sentence, its current resolution, and how the structure shows itself.

What interests me is that the subjects change completely—sunlight, rivers, seeds, clocks, recipes—but the sentences repeatedly accomplish a few recognisable things: establish an order, constitute a whole, change what things are to one another, or show how something remains available through support.

Here is how I see the structure becoming visible in each sentence, using the current resolutions. The names sometimes capture that achievement more immediately than others.

A-001 · Corpus A

22 — Sequential link

The morning sun reaches mountain villages long before their valleys receive warmth.

Candidate

22 — Sequential link

Relating × Distributed × Standing

Neither arrival is the completed point. The sentence leaves us with their ordered separation: this happens substantially before that. The relation stretches across the arrivals, and the ordering itself remains established.

“Sequential link” is especially apt here. It names what the sentence does with its contributions, rather than what either contribution describes. Remove the ordering and we have two occurrences, but lose the sentence’s point.

A-010 · Corpus A

20 — Pivoted balance

A grandmother's recipe survives because somebody remembers what cannot be written down.

Candidate

20 — Pivoted balance

Relating × Focal × Bearing

The recipe remains available through a particular remembering support. Its survival is not exhausted by retaining a document or a title.

“Pivoted balance” suggests something held through its bearing on another contribution. But this is one of the less transparent names: the sentence establishes support, not equal opposing forces. The conjunction communicates the structure more precisely—the particular recipe’s preservation holds through remembered knowledge.

What is striking is that the unwritable is not presented as a deficiency outside the recipe. It is part of what must remain available for this preservation to hold.
